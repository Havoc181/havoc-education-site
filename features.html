WHAT IT DOES TODAY

In active development on Meta Quest 3. Everything below is in the current build; items marked new were added this week and are being tested now.

Scenarios and people

  • Two scenarios: breathless older patients at home, with a family member
  • Start from the dispatch information only, like a real job
  • Take a history out loud: patient and family answer in their own (synthetic) voices
  • Breathless patients speak in short phrases and ask to sit forward
  • Clues in the home: medicines, pillows, inhalers

Looking at the patient

  • Posture and work of breathing, including tripod positioning
  • New: patients found in different positions: sitting, tripod (slight to marked), slumped back against the sofa, flat on their back or front, on either side, in the recovery position, or standing and choking
  • Skin colour, cyanosis (including the tongue), recession, raised JVP
  • Respectful exposure: lift or remove the top, roll up trousers, ask to lean forward
  • New: exposure down to underwear and never further: top off, trousers rolled up or off
  • Patients of different ages and skin tones

Hands-on kit

  • Stethoscope: 14 listening spots, sounds in time with the patient's breathing
  • Fob watch for counting
  • Patient monitor: SpO2 and pleth, NIBP, ECG, capnography (EtCO2 and RR)
  • New: monitor side pouches that unzip and open like the real ones, with the sensors stored inside and cables running from the pouches to the patient
  • New: nasal/oral capnography adapter fitted to the patient's face (in-line airway adapter to follow)
  • 4-lead and 12-lead ECG with highlighted electrode positions and a printed 12-lead
  • Tympanic thermometer, blood glucose meter with lancets and strips, peak flow meter
  • New: these now live in a zipped observations pouch with their consumables, beside a 0.6 L sharps bin
  • New: carry the monitor, the observations pouch and the sharps bin to wherever the patient is
  • Either hand; readings only appear when each step is done properly

Learning and teaching

  • Debrief: what was there to find, what you found, and why it matters
  • New: NEWS2 tallied from the first set of observations, with how it changed over the job
  • New: sicker presentations: peri-arrest rhythms, oxygen saturations that can't be read when perfusion is poor, a falling temperature
  • Instructor app on a laptop: live view, make the patient better or worse, interruptions, hints
  • Standalone and offline: no internet, accounts or patient data
  • Comfort options: seated play, kneeling, comfort vignette

WHAT'S NEXT

Being designed or built now, roughly in this order. Plans can change as we test with clinicians and educators.

Feeling for a pulse

A way to show what you'd feel at the wrist or neck, like a pulse waveform, so you can judge rate, rhythm and volume.

Treatment and response

Starting with oxygen, and the patient's observations changing in response.

More scenarios and patients

A wider range of presentations and a library of patients, with variety within each scenario.

Debrief options

Minimal, supportive and detailed debriefs built from the same session log.

Kit on request

Ask for the kit you want, the way a crew would, and have it brought in: response bag, oxygen and colour-coded pouches.

More places to find patients

Less common but real positions, such as wedged after a fall or slumped on the toilet, and patients on a bed or trolley.

Beyond the headset

A tutorial for first-time users, and a desktop version for people who can't use VR.
